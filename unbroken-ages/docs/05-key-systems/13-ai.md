# 5.13 AI v2: expansion, builders, war and peace

**Code:** [`SimpleAi.cs`](../../src/UnbrokenAges.Sim/AI/SimpleAi.cs) (the controller) and [`AiPlanners.cs`](../../src/UnbrokenAges.Sim/AI/AiPlanners.cs)
(`ExpansionAi`, `BuilderAi`, `MilitaryAi`) · tests [`AiTests.cs`](../../tests/UnbrokenAges.Tests/AiTests.cs)

The AI plays by the same rules as a person: every planner returns ordinary commands that go through validation. It runs only on the host
(and in single player), so it never has to be lockstep-deterministic across machines. It is deterministic anyway (id and tile order, no
randomness), so soaks and tests are reproducible. The full layered architecture (strategic director, operational and tactical layers) is in
[02 §AI](../02-technical-architecture.md). This is the second baseline: enough to expand, develop, fight and make peace believably.

## Production priorities (per idle city)
1. The Divergence's own buildings and Ascension project, once committed.
2. **At war:** the strongest buildable land unit, up to 2 per city + 2.
3. **A settler**, one at a time, while below `4 + era` cities and a site exists.
4. **A builder**, one at a time, while a tile needs improving.
5. A peacetime army of 1 per city + 2.
6. The best-scoring specialty district (the same planner as the player's placement lens).
7. Otherwise another soldier.

## Expansion (`ExpansionAi`)
* **Site score** = 3·Food + 2·Production + Gold over the first ring (ignoring foreign tiles), +4 per resource, +4 fresh water, +2 coast.
* A settler takes the best site within 9 tiles at score − 2 per tile of travel, avoiding sites another settler has claimed, and founds
  on arrival. It also founds early if where it stands scores within 2 points of the target. The first city is founded in place.
* Outposts: Waymakers plant one wherever it is legal.

## Builders (`BuilderAi`)
The best job within 10 tiles, on a city-owned tile with no improvement or district: value = 3·Food + 3·Production + 2·Gold + 2·(other yields)
of the improvement, +6 when it is the improvement for the tile's resource, ×2, minus the distance. The builder walks there and builds.
Power improvements are left to a later planner.

## War (`MilitaryAi`)
* **Declaring war:** from turn 30, only if not already at war with another civilization and with at least three military units. The target
  is the weakest neighbour (a city within 14 tiles) whose health-weighted strength our army outweighs by 3 : 2, if the treaty rules allow it.
* **Odds** = expected damage dealt as a share of the defender's health, against expected damage taken as a share of ours, in percent.
  Ranged attacks, civilian captures and certain kills with no risk score 100.
* **Each unit:** take the best attack with odds **above 60%**. Otherwise, if **wounded** (under 40 HP, or under 70 HP and out of supply),
  fall back to the nearest own city (a certain kill is still taken). Otherwise march to the free tile next to the nearest enemy city
  (ranged units stop within range). Hostile tiles are collected once per turn, so this stays cheap.
* **Peace:** accept an offer unless clearly winning (twice the enemy's strength, war under 30 turns). Propose peace when weaker than the
  enemy, or after 30 turns of war, once the minimum war length has passed.

## Governors and upgrades
Seat idle governors in the largest ungoverned cities, appoint with spare titles, then promote. Upgrade units with the standard variant,
keeping 100 gold in reserve.

## Survival in the opening
Until turn 150 the AI leaves a rival's last city alone, garrison included (striking the defenders would take the city), and
accepts peace from a rival down to one city. Together with the loyalty rules (a last city never revolts; a fallen capital moves to
the largest city left), no civilization is erased in the opening. Four-player soaks: all four alive at turn 150 on every seed tried.

Later, a civilization reduced to two cities or fewer (having lost one) makes a **last stand**: +8 city strength and +5 for units
defending in its own territory. The AI does not declare war on a remnant, nor on a rival already fighting another great power
(no dogpiles); a remnant starts no wars; and after 15 turns of war the AI makes peace with a remnant. Four-player games at turn
300: 3.25 civilizations standing on average (2.0 before).
