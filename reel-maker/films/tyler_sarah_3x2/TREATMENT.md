# THE GARDEN, BEFORE AND AFTER
### Tyler & Sarah · an engagement film · 3:2 · ~4:10

*Director's treatment, written for the footage that exists: Sony FX3, S-Log3, 3840×2160 at 29.97p, shot on 31 July 2026 in a redwood-and-oak garden. The garden has a stone waterfall, terraced ivy, brick paths, hydrangeas and Edison string lights hanging from the oaks. The camera clip numbers (`0437`–`0503`) are used throughout, so an editor can pull every shot directly.*

---

## 1 · Logline & emotional thesis

**A garden is prepared by many hands for a single question. Then it fills with everyone who loves the two people who answered it.**

**Thesis.** This is not a highlight reel. It's a film about the *before* and the *after* of one moment.

- **The moment itself:** we see it the way the people who were there saw it: from a distance, in real time, without a cut.
- **The after:** we stay so close to the couple that the world drops out of focus.

The garden is the third character. We meet it empty and quiet. We watch it get dressed in roses. We leave it ringing with laughter.

**The couple's energy:** quiet intensity breaking into helpless joy.

- **Sarah** laughs with her whole body and touches his face constantly. Her hands are the film's recurring motif.
- **Tyler** is steady and sure, and visibly fighting tears.

## 2 · Visual & tonal bible

### Palette

The garden already has the palette, so we protect it rather than invent one.

| Role | Hex | Source in frame |
|---|---|---|
| Deep shadow (never pure black) | `#1C241E` | oak trunks, under-canopy |
| Ivy / foliage body | `#3E5638` | the terraced ivy that is behind them in half the film |
| Rose red (the only saturated colour, held back until it matters) | `#8C1C25` | the roses, the petal path |
| Burgundy | `#5B1F28` | Tyler's suit (it rhymes with the roses) |
| Cream | `#E8DECB` | Sarah's dress, the silk rose at her shoulder |
| Skin anchor | `#C99478` | warm, never orange |
| Practical gold | `#E6B868` | the string-light bulbs, the ring |
| Paper highlight | `#F3EBDC` | sky through the redwoods, rolled off and never white |

**Film-stock reference.** Kodak Vision3 250D printed to 2383, with a lean toward Portra 400's soft greens and cream highlights. Reds stay dense and slightly dark, like Wong Kar-wai's *In the Mood for Love*, rather than candy red.

### Lighting philosophy

Overcast, late-afternoon, natural light only. It's a giant softbox under the redwood canopy, and we treat it as a gift. Faces get soft, wrap-around light and the ivy goes rich and dark.

- **The rule:** the background is always half a stop under the faces. In the grade we place faces at roughly 45 IRE, and let the garden fall away around them in a soft vignette that follows the subject.
- **The string lights:** they're our only practical. We hold a few shots purely for their bokeh.

### Lens language

The footage is spherical. We create a sense of focal-length change by reframing inside the 4K frame, which gives roughly a 1.5× punch-in at 3:2.

- **Wides (feel of a 24–28mm, deep focus):** used only for the garden and for the proposal itself. The couple are small figures inside something bigger.
- **Mid (feel of a 40–50mm):** family, preparation and hands. This is honest, documentary distance.
- **Close (feel of an 85mm, shallow):** everything after "yes". Faces fill the 3:2 frame off-centre, with negative space toward the person they're looking at.
- **"Anamorphic feel":** we never fake flares or ovals. The feel comes from horizontal composition alone. We use the 3:2 width to put two faces on the thirds with the ivy blurred between them.

### Texture & grain

- **Grain:** a fine, 35mm-scale grain, stronger in the mid-tones and lighter in the shadows.
- **Halation:** a whisper of it on the string-light bulbs and on the cream dress against dark ivy.
- **No sharpening halos.** The FX3 is already clinical, and our job is to make it feel remembered.

### Pacing philosophy

The film breathes in **three tempos**:

1. **Still (the garden):** locked-off frames held 6–9 seconds, with ambient sound only.
2. **Real time (the truth):** preparation, the proposal and the family. Cuts come on action and on sound, and nothing is retimed.
3. **Dream (the two of them):** footage conformed from 29.97 to 23.976, which gives an 80 % natural slow motion with no interpolation artefacts. A few moments are step-printed (see §7). Cuts come on breath, not on beat.

## 4 · Editing timeline & structure

```
0:00  ░░ PROLOGUE: the garden, empty        ambient only (water, birds, wind)
0:38  ▒▒ I  · many hands                    piano enters, sparse; real-time cuts on action
1:20  ▓  II · the wide wait (time-dissolves) music thins to a held note → silence
1:35  ██ II · THE QUESTION (real time, uncut) diegetic only: distance, gasps, cheer
2:05  ▓▓ III· everyone                      cheer carries over the cut, strings lift
2:35  ██ IV · them (80 % dream tempo)       full theme, intimate; voices low under music
3:15     ↳ the ring                         MUSIC DROPS OUT for ~3 s, breath + laugh only
3:25  ▓▓ V  · family, the path rhymes       theme returns brighter, real time
3:50  ░░ CODA · the kneel remembered        strings resolve; piano alone
4:05     title · fade · water sound tail    end ≈ 4:10
```

- **Opening hook:** no faces for 38 seconds. The hook is *sound and patience*. The first red, on the roses at about 0:52, lands like a heartbeat because we've been starved of colour.
- **Rising arc:** many hands, then one man alone, then the wide wait. The tension comes from *stillness*, not tempo.
- **Peaks:**
  1. The answer, wide and in real time, about 1:50.
  2. Foreheads together, about 2:58.
  3. The ring in silence, about 3:15.
- **Breathing room:** the prologue, the 0461 time-dissolves, and the coda.
- **Cut grammar:**
  - **Hard cuts** on action and sound in the real-time acts.
  - **Long dissolves of 1–2 s** for time passing and in the dream act.
  - **Match cuts** on the path, three times, and on the red, from roses to petals to burgundy.
  - **One smash to black of 0.5 s** after the proposal, so the cheer enters before the picture.
- **Where slow motion lives:** only Act IV and the coda, the places where *we* are allowed to linger and the world isn't.
- **Where real time lives:** preparation, the proposal and the family. Truth never gets slowed down.

## 5 · Sound design & music direction

- **Temperature:** warm, restrained, modern-classical. The references are:
  - Dustin O'Halloran's *Opus 23*
  - Ólafur Arnalds' *Saman*
  - Max Richter's *On the Nature of Daylight* (for the shape, not the sadness)
  - Hildur Guðnadóttir's string textures (used sparingly)
- **Instruments:** felt piano and a small string section. The cellos carry the theme, and violins only arrive in Act IV.
- **The score in this repo:** an original piece built around the edit (`films/tyler_sarah_3x2/score.py`). It's in **D♭ major**, with a 4-note motif that rises a third and falls back, like a question and its answer.
- **Swells & drops:**
  - **0:00–0:38:** none. Water, birds, leaves.
  - **0:38:** a single piano note on the first footstep of 1.1.
  - **0:52:** the motif on the first roses.
  - **1:20:** the motif stretches out and leaves one held cello note.
  - **1:35:** **the music is gone.** Only the camera's diegetic audio of the proposal remains.
  - **2:05:** the strings enter under the cheer.
  - **2:35:** full theme.
  - **3:15:** **drop to silence** for the ring.
  - **3:25:** the theme returns, brighter.
  - **3:50:** piano alone, resolving.
  - **4:05:** the water sound returns from the prologue.
- **Diegetic moments (from the real camera audio):**
  - footsteps on brick
  - petals and paper being handled
  - fabric as he shrugs into the jacket
  - the distant gasp and cheer
  - Sarah's laugh in Act IV, kept faintly under the music at about −24 dB
- **Key silences:** after the proposal cut (0.5 s of black, before sound), and the ring.

## 6 · Colour grade & finishing notes

The pipeline is implemented in `film.py`.

- **Primary look:** S-Log3/S-Gamut3.Cine developed to display, then a Vision3 250D → 2383 print-style emulation. Contrast is **gentle at the toe and soft at the shoulder**, never crunchy.
- **Normalisation first:** every shot is exposure- and white-balance-matched to a shared target before the look is applied, so the 70+ clips read as one afternoon.
- **Contrast curve:**
  - blacks lifted to about 3 % (a print-like base)
  - mid-tone contrast moderate
  - highlights roll off from about 70 % with a long filmic shoulder, so the sky through the redwoods is cream paper, not white
- **Skin first:** a vibrance-style saturation boost that protects skin tones. Skin sits at about 45–55 IRE and is never pushed orange.
- **Greens:** foliage is pulled slightly toward olive and darkened about ⅓ stop, so the faces win.
- **Reds:** rose red and the burgundy suit get density, not brightness.
- **Split tone:** warm-cream highlights (`#F3EBDC`) and neutral-to-faintly-green shadows (`#1C241E`).
- **LUT starting point:** the custom grade is baked into a **33³ 3D LUT** per shot, which carries that shot's matching. Any editor can reproduce it in Resolve by applying a Vision3/2383 film-emulation LUT after a CST from S-Log3 to Rec.709, then softening the shoulder.
- **Texture:** 35 mm-scale temporal grain (stronger in the mids), a light vignette and soft halation on the brightest practicals.

## 7 · Bells & whistles

1. **The path rhyme:** the same brick path, three times: empty (P4), dressed with petals (1.5), and with the couple standing on it (5.1). The film's structure, told by one location.
2. **The unflinching wide:** the proposal plays in one real-time wide from the 13-minute locked-off take. There are no cut-ins. Restraint as the special effect.
3. **Time-lapse by dissolve:** the wait before the proposal is compressed with three long dissolves from the same locked frame, as people drift in and out like ghosts.
4. **Step-printing (Wong Kar-wai):**
   - **The technique:** the dream sequences are conformed to 80 % speed, and selected beats are *step-printed*, holding each frame for 2–3 frames with blending. The result is the smeared, longing motion of *Chungking Express* and *In the Mood for Love*.
   - **Why it suits this footage:** it's achievable from 30p without interpolation artefacts, and the motion blur reads as memory.
5. **Hands motif:** friends' hands making, her hand on his face, then her hand with the ring. Three beats, never stated.
6. **The colour drought:** the prologue keeps saturation slightly lower, so the first roses hit like a pulse.
7. **Sound before picture:** the cheer arrives over black, and the water from the prologue returns over the end card.
8. **Subject-aware vignette and reframing:** the 3:2 crop and the vignette centre follow the two faces in Act IV, so the light seems to find them.
9. **One silence:** the ring plays with no music at all.

## 8 · Final delivery specs

- **Aspect:** exactly **3:2**.
- **Master:** **2160 × 1440** (cropped from the UHD 3840 × 2160 source as a 3240 × 2160 window, which leaves room to reframe).
- **Web:** 1620 × 1080, H.264 High, about 16 Mbps, AAC 256 kbps.
- **Frame rate:** a **23.976 fps** master.
  - **Real-time sections** are frame-rate-converted from 29.97 to 23.976. This keeps true speed and gives a film cadence.
  - **Dream sections** conform 29.97 to 23.976 frame-for-frame, for 80 % slow motion that's artefact-free.
  - A 29.97 master is also possible if the client prefers buttery motion. Then the dream sections use step-printing only.
- **Text:** deliberately almost none. One title, **"Tyler & Sarah"**, in small Playfair Display Italic. It's placed in the lower-third negative space of the final canopy shot, with a 1.5 s fade-in and fade-out. No dates, quotes or captions. The images say it.
- **Audio:** stereo, mastered to about −16 LUFS integrated with −1 dBTP peaks. The music bed is mixed under the diegetic audio, and silence is treated as an instrument.
